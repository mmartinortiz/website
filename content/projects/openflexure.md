This year I attended [FOSDEM](https://fosdem.org) in Brussels, and I fell in love with the stand where the people from [OpenFlexure](https://openflexure.org/) were showing off their microscope.

A few months later, with some spare time and a 3D printer in need of a project, I gathered all the required bits and bolts from [TLab](https://taulab.eu/3-openflexure) and set aside a weekend for printing and assembly.

The version I built is the V7 Low Resolution, using a Raspberry Pi 3 and a NoIR camera. Once assembled, everything worked out of the box, and we could examine how leaves look at a magnification of 20×.

Don't hesitate to build one yourself if you've always wanted to explore the world at a different scale. Leaves, water, insects — the list of everyday objects that can be observed with this microscope is endless. I uploaded a [3MF project to Printables](https://www.printables.com/model/1813373-openflexure-microscope-v7-low-resolution) with all parts distributed across 3 different beds, so they can easily be printed with different colours or materials.

And if 20× isn't enough for you, you can always start with the [High Resolution](https://build.openflexure.org/openflexure-microscope/v7.0.0-beta5/high_res_microscope.html), or upgrade your low-resolution build with the required parts.
